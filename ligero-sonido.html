A LIGHT SOUND

Deconstruction is an analytical practice that dismantles an object's initial concept. This sculpture deconstructs the structural order of the city, transforming it into a dislocated system of pipes and valves designed for the flow of sound.

The work is rooted in research into Bogotá's soundscapes and aqueduct systems, featuring in-situ audio recordings of water in different contexts. These sounds resonate deep within the pipes and can only be heard by approaching a funnel at one end, where viewers can actively modulate the volume by opening and closing the valves.

Blending geometry and color, the installation creates an abstract communication system. By requiring physical proximity to perceive the hidden audio, the piece demands an intimate engagement, shifting the viewer's traditional relationship with both the artwork and the exhibition space.

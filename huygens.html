HUYGENS' PRINCIPLE

When a stone is thrown into water, concentric circles ripple outward. If two stones are dropped nearby, their waves collide and merge—a phenomenon shared by all wave types, including light and sound.

Dutch physicist Christiaan Huygens postulated that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary waves, spreading in all directions at the same speed.

This principle serves as a profound mechanism to trace wave propagation, beautifully explaining the complex visual phenomena of light refraction and diffraction.

To explore this, I developed an interactive program simulating Huygens' principle. Each click creates a disturbance that divides into four points, representing the dimensions of light propagation: length, width, depth, and time.

As these points multiply, they generate intricate geometric patterns. However, an excess of interactions collapses this simplicity into interference—mirroring 'incoherent light'—where the superposition of states disperses until only pure chaos survives.
